permitting and facilitating the Private Hospital to charge exorbitant medical bills
from the patients.



Respondent submitted that a point-wise reply along with relevant information
has already been provided to the Appellant. In response to Appellant's
contentions, he explained that as per the extant norms, the Hospital is entitled
to apply for registration 3 months prior to the date of expiry of its registration
or thereafter within 3 months of its expiry, in the meantime registration of
Hospital is treated as valid. The Artemis Hospital had applied for extension of its
registration certificate, which was lying pending with the competent authority,
therefore, the Hospital is treated to be having deemed registration certificate
on the date of hospitalisation of the patient. However, the date given in the
registration certificate is the date of issuance of certificate which may be
counted from the date of application of registration. He further apprised the
Commission as far as claim of Appellant is concerned, there is no provisions in
the Delhi Nursing Home Registration Act & Rules which may empower the
Respondent to compel any private Hospital to provide cashless treatment to the
patient.



The Appellant contested that the averments of the Respondent made just now
is something esoteric and completely hidden from public knowledge and
therefore, is a private and illegal arrangement between them for issuing licenses
from back date or retrospective effect. This Public Authority is a regulator and
having control over the activities of the private Hospitals and is competent to
cancel the license of the private hospital upon taking cognizance of their
malpractices as pointed out by the Appellant.



By summing up his arguments, the Appellant's representative again prayed that
since correct/complete information has not been given by the PIO against point
Nos.1 to 9 of the RTI Application, therefore, the PIO may be directed to provide

## Order on 13.06.2024:




The Commission, after adverting to the facts and circumstances of the case,
hearing both the parties at length and perusal of the records, observes that the
core contention of the Appellant in the instant Appeal was non-receipt of correct
and accurate information from the PIO and also arbitrary practice adopted by
the Artemis Hospital in raising unjustified demands/ bills during hospitalization
which gets facilitated by respondent's clandestine practice of renewing the
licence with retrospective effect as a matter of routine. In response to it, the
Respondent claimed that point-wise reply has been provided to the Appellant
and further, that the Respondent Public Authority has no authority to direct the
private hospital to give cashless treatments to the patients. In addition to it,
Respondent explained that plea of Appellant that Artemis Hospital not
possessing valid registration certificate at the relevant time is not acceptable
since as per their normal practice the registration certificate under process is
deemed to be considered as a fact that registration of Hospital is still valid.



The Commission considering the submission of the parties concluded that the
reply furnished by the PIO in the first instance and the submission given during
the course of hearing are contradictory. This casual conduct of the PIO causes
unwarranted obstructions to the Appellant's right to information and is a grave
violation to the provisions of the 
RTI Act
.



In view of the above, the then PIO, Dr. Nitin Kumar and the present PIO, Shri
Sandeep Kumar Agarwal are show caused as to why punitive action under

Section 20(1)
 should not be initiated against them and also why compensation
should not be awarded to the Appellant. The then PIO and the present PIO are
required to submit their written statements both through post and via uploading
on http://dsscic.nic.in/online-link-paper-compliance/add within four weeks
from the date of receiving this order. Shri Sandeep Kumar Agarwal, Medical
Superintendent, Nursing Home Cell, the present PIO to serve a copy of this order
to Shri Nitin Kumar, the then PIO.

## Decision:




The Commission, upon a perusal of the facts on record as well as on the basis of
the proceedings during the hearing at the outset adversely viewed the conduct
of Shri Sandeep Kumar Agarwal, Medical Superintendent, Delhi Health Services,
Delhi who being the then and the present PIO in this case hardly bothered to
give any written explanation/submission in compliance of the order dated
07.10.2024 and casually opted to sidetrack his responsibility by merely
forwarding the hearing notice to the Medical Superintendent, Artemis Lite
Hospital and appeared with the intention to merely mark his presence before
the Commission in person. Upon being asked during the hearing regarding the
status of revised reply, Shri Sandeep Kumar Agarwal, Medical Superintendent

RTI Act

In view of the above, the Commission is of the considered view that the

 have been clearly violated by Shri Sandeep Kumar

Notwithstanding the above, it is noted from the oral submission of the
Respondent that registration of hospitals is being renewed retrospectively,
which essentially means that private hospitals have an option to withdraw
renewal requests should they get caught in malpractices. Despite repeated
directions from the Commission during pendency of this case, Respondent has
not uploaded the relevant legal/executive order authorizing such a back dated/
ex post facto issuance.



In a number of judgements on the issue of environmental clearance which also
affect the life of the people, the Courts have repeatedly said that clearances
cannot be granted retrospectively. Environmental impacts of any project can
affect the life of citizens because it impacts air and water- two essential
prerequisites for the very life. Here the hospitals deal directly with the life of the
people and hence, the same principles apply to them too.



In a true spirit of upfront disclosure and consultation with the impacted (or likely
to be impacted) citizens, the environmental laws mandate holding
environmental public hearing by an independent panel prior to grant of
clearance. However, in case of renewal of hospital license, despite complaints
of malpractices and repeated RTI applications the affected people are not given
a chance of being heard at the time of renewal of license of the private hospitals.



In view of the above, an advisory under 
Section 25(5)
 of the RTI Act is issued to
the Respondent Public Authority to amend the applicable Regulations governing
renewal of licenses of private hospitals so as to afford an opportunity of hearing

practice of issuing ex post facto renewal of license.
