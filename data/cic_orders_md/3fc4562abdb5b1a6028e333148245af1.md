## ORDER


## FACTS:


The Appellant vide his RTI application sought information on 18 points regarding when a person
gets the Mediclaim / Health policy from OICL organization; terms and conditions on the basis of
which any TPA had been appointed; under which rule or order M/s Good Health Plan Ltd. was
allotted TPA with his policy; what time frame had been decided for TPA to clear the hospital
bill, pre hospitalization bill and post hospitalization bill to the claimant and other issues thereto.



The CPIO, vide its letter dated 20.08.2018 provided a point - wise response to the Appellant.
Dissatisfied by the response of the CPIO, the Appellant approached the FAA. The FAA, vide its
## order dated 19.09.2018 informed that RTI application had already been replied by CPIO vide

letter dated 20.08.2018 and upheld the response of the CPIO.

## HEARING:


## Facts emerging during the hearing:


## The following were present:


## Appellant: Mr. Aditya Nashier through TC:


Respondent: Mr. Ravinder Singh Rawat, RM & CPIO and Mr. Anil Aggarawal, through TC:



The Appellant reiterated the contents of the RTI application and stated that complete and
satisfactory information was not provided by the Respondent. During the hearing, the Appellant
read out the contents of his RTI application in seriatim and submitted that the response on all
points except point nos 01 and 06 was incorrect and misleading. The Appellant specifically
addressed the issue of non-transparency in the process followed by the TPA in settlement of the
policy claims and stated that no payment was made for the claim made by him citing justifiable
reasons for the same. In its reply, the Respondent stated that point wise information was
provided by the CPIO which was also concurred by the FAA. On being queried by the
Commission regarding the terms and conditions for the appointment of TPA, policy followed by
the TPAs for settlement of claim, rule allowing to provide list of TPAs to the applicant for
selecting the TPA, etc was uploaded on the website or not, the Respondent feigned ignorance
and consistently maintained that all such details were available in the insurance document signed
by the Appellant. Examining point-wise queries raised by the Appellant and the response of the
Respondent, it was evident that there was a lack of clarity in a critical and sensitive matter like
settlement of health insurance claims and the role of the TPA in such matters. It was noted by the
Commission that there were a number of grey areas that needed to be resolved distinctly in all
such matters for the benefit of the public at large so that each of the clauses contained therein and
its ramifications were understood by the policy holder in a clear, precise and cogent manner.
The Commission noted that in the aforesaid case the reply given by the Respondent was evasive,
misleading and confusing resulting in unnecessary interpretations by the examining authorities
which was unwarranted.



The Commission was in receipt of a written submission from the Respondent dated 16.04.2020
wherein the reply of the CPIO/ FAA was re-iterated.



Having heard both the parties and on perusal of available records, the Commission observed that
a voluntary disclosure of all information that ought to be displayed in the public domain should
be the rule and members of public who having to seek information should be an exception. An
open government, which is the cherished objective of the 
RTI Act
, can be realised only if all
public offices comply with proactive disclosure norms. 
Section 4(2)
 of the RTI Act mandates
every public authority to provide as much information suo-motu to the public at regular intervals
through various means of communications, including the Internet, so that the public need not
resort to the use of 
RTI Act
.



The Hon'ble Supreme Court of India in the matter of 
CBSE and Anr. Vs. Aditya Bandopadhyay
and Ors
## 2011 (8) SCC 497 held as under:



       "37. The right to information is a cherished right. Information and right to information
       are intended to be formidable tools in the hands of responsible citizens to fight corruption
       and to bring in transparency and accountability. The provisions of 
RTI Act
 should be
       enforced strictly and all efforts should be made to bring to light the necessary

## DECISION:




Keeping in view the facts of the case and the submissions made by both the parties, the
Commission instructs the Respondent to reexamine the RTI application and provide a point wise
response to the Appellant in accordance with the provisions of the 
RTI Act, 2005
 as also suo
motu disclose in unambiguous terms, the terms and conditions for the appointment of TPA,
policy followed by the TPAs for settlement of claim, rules determining the selection and
allocation of TPAs to the policy holders etc on its website forthwith. Simultaneously, the Public
Authority must formulate FAQs in this regard in different languages within a period of 30 days
from the date of receipt of this order depending upon the condition for containment of the
Corona Virus Pandemic in the Country in the larger public interest and display it on its website
prominently for perusal by all concerned.



The Appeal stands disposed accordingly.
