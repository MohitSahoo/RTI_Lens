## Relevant facts emerging from appeal:


Based on a media report regarding partnership between the ICMR and Bharat
Biotech International Ltd executed via a formal Memorandum of Understanding
(MoU), the Appellant filed an online RTI application dated 04.05.2021 and sought
the following information with regard to the COVAXIN vaccine:

  1. Copy of Memorandum of Understanding between the ICMR and the Bharat
     Biotech
  2. The amount of money spent on the development of the vaccine
  3. The royalty which ICMR would receive from the sale of COVAXIN

  CPIO vide online disposal on 06.05.2021 denied the information with regard to
 point No. 1 & 2
 u/s 8(1)(j)
 of the RTI Act, 2005. With regard to point No.3, the
 appellant was informed that royalty of ICMR would be 5% on net sales.

 Dissatisfied with the response received from the PIO, the appellant filed a first
## appeal on 07.05.2021. The FAA vide order dated 07.05.2021 disposed of the

## appeal on line with the following remarks:


       The reply of the CPIO is satisfactory and in order. Third Party confidential
       information can't be shared under 
RTI Act
. The CPIO has already shared the
       other available information.


Feeling aggrieved and dissatisfied, the Appellant approached the Commission with
the instant Second Appeal.

## Facts emerging during the hearing:



In order to ensure social distancing and prevent the spread of the pandemic,
COVID-19, hearing through audio conference was scheduled after giving prior
notice to both the parties.



The Appellant participated in the hearing through audio conference. She stated
that the information sought pertained to a formal MOU executed between the
ICMR (a Public Authority) and Bharat Biotech International Limited (hereafter
referred as Bharat Biotech) and exemption
 u/s 8 (1) (j)
 of the RTI Act was
incorrectly claimed by the Respondent. While stating that disclosure of information
did not involve any breach of privacy, the Appellant stated that even if it was
assumed that the information sought may result in violation of commercial interest
or trade secrets of a third party, the information has to be disclosed since ICMR is
a government body and Bharat Biotech is a company which does not fall within the
definition of an individual having privacy rights. Moreover, the issues raised in the
RTI application pertained to the larger public interest in the current situation.
During the hearing, she also stated that the terms of an MoU between Microsoft
Corporation (I) Private Limited and the Ministry of Agriculture were disclosed while
replying to another RTI application recently thus similar information sought in the
present matter should also be disclosed. 
In support of her contentions, she also
referred to the judgements of the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi in 
Jamia Milia
Islamiya vs Sh Ikramuddin WP (C) No
 5677/2011 and of the Jharkhand High
Court in 
State of Jharkhand vs Navin Kumar Singha
 AIR 2008 Jharkhand 19.




The Respondent represented by Dr R Lakshminarayanan, DDG (Admn), ICMR
participated in the hearing through audio conference. While admitting that
exemption
 u/s 8(1)(j)
 of the RTI Act was incorrectly claimed earlier, Dr
Lakshminarayanan stated that the document sought is not a public document and
is a highly sensitive agreement between the ICMR and Bharat Biotech having
commercial implications and can be used by other competing pharmaceutical
companies for their commercial interests. Thus, the information sought is
exempted
 u/s 8 (1) (d)
 of the RTI Act.

 On being queried by the Commission if the agreement/ MOU/ contract is still in
operation, Dr R Lakshminarayanan replied in the affirmative. The Appellant
however stated that in case whole of the information cannot be disclosed, part of it
which does not have any major commercial implication can be severed
 u/s 10
 and
provided by the Respondent.



## Decision


Having heard both the parties and on perusal of the available records, the
Commission at the outset sets aside the reply of the CPIO where exemption
 u/s 8
(1) (j)
 of the RTI Act, 2005 was claimed. It is evident that the reply was erroneous
since the Appellant did not seek any personal information the disclosure of which
had any relationship with any public activity or interest or which would cause
unwarranted invasion to the privacy of an individual. However, the Respondent
during the hearing claimed exemption
 u/s 8 (1) (d)
 of the RTI Act and stated that
disclosure of information could have major commercial implications. It was also
stated that the agreement is still in operation. The Commission concurs with the
view of the Respondent and observes that the information sought would fall under
the ambit of the exemption
 u/s 8 (1) (d)
 of the RTI Act, 2005. Moreover, since the
contract is still in operation and as stated by the Respondent contains
commercially sensitive terms and conditions, the Commission is not inclined to
direct disclosure of information applying severability clause
 u/s 10
 of the RTI Act
at this stage.



The Hon'ble High Court of Jharkhand in State of Jharkhand vs Naveen Kumar
Singha and Anr, AIR 2008 Jharkhand 19 dated 08.08.2017 held as under


  "In our considered opinion a contract entered into by the public authority with a
  private person cannot be treated as confidential after completion of contract."



Hence, in the light of the above observations, no further intervention of the
Commission is required in the matter. The instant Second Appeal stands disposed
off accordingly.
