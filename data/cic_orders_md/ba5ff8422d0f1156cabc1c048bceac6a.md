## ORDER


## FACTS:


The Appellant vide his RTI application sought information on 22 points regarding copy of
Memorandum of Association of NBE Welfare Fund; copy of rules and regulations of NBE
Welfare Fund, copy of Minutes of Meeting of NBE Welfare Fund from 2014 to 2019; list of
loans disbursed with names of beneficiaries of NBE Welfare Fund for the period 2014 to 2019;
list of persons drawing salary from NBE Welfare Fund during the period 2018 to 2019, etc.

The CPIO, vide its letter dated 23.07.2018 provided point wise response to the Appellant.
Dissatisfied by the response of the CPIO on points 06, 14, 15 and 16, the Appellant approached
the FAA. The FAA, vide its order dated 01.09.2018 stated that the reply given by CPIO, NBE to
the Appellant was based on available documents with the concerned department.

The Commission observed that the 
RTI Act, 2005
 stipulates time limits in its various provisions
relating to responding to RTI Applications, transfer of applications, filing and disposing of first
## appeal to ensure that a culture of information dissemination is strengthened so that a robust

functioning of the democracy gets established. This was recognised by the Hon'ble High Court
of Delhi in 
# Mujibur Rehman vs Central Information Commission (W.P. (C

) 3845/2007)(Dated
## 28 April, 2009) wherein it was held as under:





       "14.......The court cannot be unmindful of the circumstances under which the Act was
       framed, and brought into force. It seeks to foster an "openness culture" among state
       agencies, and a wider section of "public authorities" whose actions have a significant or
       lasting impact on the people and their lives. Information seekers are to be furnished
       what they ask for, unless the Act prohibits disclosure; they are not to be driven away
       through sheer inaction or filibustering tactics of the public authorities or their officers.
       It is to ensure these ends that time limits have been prescribed, in absolute terms, as
       well as penalty provisions. These are meant to ensure a culture of information
       disclosure so necessary for a robust and functioning democracy."

## DECISION:




Keeping in view the facts of the case and the submissions made by both the parties and in the
light of the observations made in the preceding paragraphs, the Commission instructs the CPIO
to allow inspection of records sought in the RTI application to the Appellant on a mutually
convenient date and time and provide photocopies of the selected documents as per the RTI
Rules, 2012, within a period of 30 days from the date of receipt of this order depending upon the
condition for containment of the Corona Virus Pandemic in the Country, as agreed. The FAA is
instructed to ensure compliance of the said directive to the CPIO.



The Commission also instructs the Respondent Public Authority to convene periodic
conferences/seminars to sensitize, familiarize and educate the concerned officials about the
relevant provisions of the 
RTI Act, 2005
 for effective discharge of its duties and responsibilities.



The Appeal stands disposed accordingly.

With regard to power conferred on the Commission regarding enforcement / execution of its own
## orders, in terms of the provisions contained in the

RTI Act
, the Commission referred to the
## decision of the Hon'ble High Court of Karnataka at Bangalore in the matter of

Sri G Basavaraju
v. Smt Arundathi & Anr.', CCC
. No. 525 of 2008 (Civil) decision dated 27.01.2009 wherein it
## was held as under:





        "9. 
Section 20
 of the RTI Act provides for penalties. It confers powers on the
        Commission on the basis of which it can enforce its order. 
The Act
 having provided for

        constitution of the Commission and the power to impose the penalties by way of levy of
       fine and also the Statutory right to recommend to the Government for disciplinary action
       against the State Information Officer, itself has the necessary powers / provisions, in the
       form of the provisions of 
Contempt of Courts Act
. It is cardinal principle of
       interpretation of Statute, well settled by catena of decisions of the Apex Court that,
       Courts or tribunals, must be held to possess power to execute its own order. 
Further,
       the RTI Act
, which is a self-contained Code, even if it has not been specifically spelt
       out, must be deemed to have been conferred upon the Commission the power in order
       to make its order effective, by having recourse to
 S. 20
.

       "10. The powers of the Commission to entertain and decide the Complaints, necessarily
       shows that, the Commission has the necessary power to adjudicate the grievances and
       decide the matters brought before it, in terms of the provisions contained in the 
RTI Act
.
       The Legislative will in incorporating 
Section 20
 in the 
RTI Act
, conferring power on
       the Commission to impose the penalties, by necessary implication is to enable the
       Commission to do everything that is indispensible for the purpose of carrying out the
       purposes in view contemplated under the Act. In our considered view, provisions of
 S.
       20
 can be exercised by the Commission also to enforce its order. The underlying object
       in empowering the Commission to impose the penalty and/or to resort to other mode

In view of the interpretation given by the High Court of Karnataka relating to 
Section 20
 of the

       18. It is well-settled that when a power is given to an authority to do something it
