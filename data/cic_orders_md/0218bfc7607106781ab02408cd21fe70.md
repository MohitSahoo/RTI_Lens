Grounds for Second Appeal
The CPIO has provided incomplete information.



Submissions made by Appellant and Respondent during Hearing:



The appellant vide his written submissions dated 25.04.2020 mentioned that in
respect of case no.CIC/DOTEL/A/2018/154406, he visited the office of the CPIO on
17.12.2018 and he was provided with the documents in respect of only points no.
2,3,5 and 6 of the RTI application. He received the same through e-mail also on
28.12.2018. However, he was not provided with the information on the rest of
 the points. He submitted that through e-mail dated 28.12.2018 he had
acknowledged the information received too.

He further submitted that he is aggrieved as his leave application for bringing his
personal vehicle from Ahmedabad to Bhopal was rejected on his transfer. He
further submitted that being aggrieved with the unreasonable attitude of the
organisation, he was compelled to file an appeal to the President of India u/s
24(1)(i)(b) of the Central Civil Services (Classification, Control and Appeal) Rules,
1965 and also a complaint to the PMO in CPGRAM portal.
Smt. Neha Singh submitted that during the period 2015 to 2016 atleast 7 to 8
court cases were filed by the applicant, and 45 grievance representations on all
the similar issue. However, nothing was pending presently and all were replied to
as per rule. She further submitted that the matter relating to TA and payments
are pending before the Central Administrative Tribunal, Jabalpur Bench which was
registered as OA no. 710. She further pleaded that the appellant is using different
fora again and again for raising the similar grievances.She also apprised the
Commission that some of the court cases have already been dismissed by CAT.
Smt. Sandhya Sameera submitted that she is dealing with the RTI applications of
the appellant, and representing the public authority in Court cases and also
handled his disciplinary case as presiding officer too. She further submitted that
the appellant's grievance can be segregated into three parts, viz Court cases,
disciplinary cases and RTI cases. She further submitted that disciplinary action was
initiated against the appellant based on some articles of charge because of which
only provisional pension is being given to him. On a query by the Commission the
CPIO submitted that the appellant availed sick leave without a proper CGHS/
medical certificate; he was on unauthorised leave and the office was unable to
contact him when his presence was required.He kept on asking for information
through RTI only to harass the public authority. She further submitted that during
the disciplinary proceedings the copy of defence documents and prosecution
documents containing 450 pages were given to the applicant, which is again
similar to the information sought in these RTI applications. She further submitted
that all the RTI applications queries were tried to be sorted out and responded to
after analysing as to what information was sought, but found that it remained
inconclusive.

 The appellant contended that he filed his 1st appeal under Central Civil Services
(Classification, Control and Appeal) Rules, 1965 on 24.07.2015 and the 15th appeal
on 27.05.2016. He pointed out that the memorandum of charges was issued to
him on 30.06.2016 after his appeals and is a plan to cause him irreparable loss. He
also drew the attention of the Commission to enclosure 7 page 30 of case no.
CIC/DOTEL/A/2018/634683 where the FAA had mentioned that the appeals and
## complaints of the appellant before the President of India and the Prime Minister

of India are pending before the SEA Section, Department of Telecommunication,
till disposal of the chargesheet pending against him under Rule 14 (CCS)(CCA). He
stressed on the fact that pendency of chargesheet has no relevance with the
## information sought by him.


His contention was that the RTI applications should not be mixed with the court
cases or the disiplinary matter. However, he failed to point out the deficiencies in
the reply other than to say that complete information was not given to him, even
after inspection.

Smt. Sandhya Sameera argued that the RTI applications filed by the appellant are
repetitive in nature, inconclusive and frivolous. She also submitted that on
17.12.2018 the appellant had availed of inspection also and some documents
were shared with him. She further submitted that as the appellant was asked to
inspect documents, he also filed another 10-15 RTI applications asking for details
of pages, number of files where the information sought are scattered etc. She
summed up stating that seeing the volume of the information sought, the CPIO is
not in a position to reply to his repeated and frivolous RTI applications which are a
drain on the resources of the respondent department.
The appellant again contested that documents given to him on 17.12.2018 were
also only information on 4 points out of 18 points, and that too only copies of his
own applications were given.

## Observations:


At the outset the Commission observed that RTI queries should be focussed to
receive a proper reply. However, by the appellant's own admission he submitted
that being aggrieved due to denial of leave etc, he had filed appeals to the
President of India, followed with complaints to the Prime Minister of India in
 CPGRAMs portal. It was found pertinent to refer to Sec 24 of the Central Civil
Services (Classification, Control and Appeal) Rules, 1965 which reads as follows:



      "24. Appellate Authority
      (1) A Government servant, including a person who has ceased to be in
      Government service, may prefer an appeal against all or any of the orders
      specified in Rule 23 to the authority specified in this behalf either in the
      Schedule or by a general or special order of the President or, where no such
      authority is specified-

             (i) where such Government servant is or was a member of a Central
             Service, Group 'A' or Group 'B' or holder of a Central Civil Post, Group
             'A' or Group 'B' - (a) to the appointing authority, where the order
## appealed against is made by an authority subordinate to it; or (b)to

             the President where such order is made by any other authority;

             (ii) where such Government servant is or was a member of a Central
             Civil Service, Group 'C' or Group 'D', or holder of a Central Civil Post,
             Group 'C' or Group 'D', to the authority to which the authority making
             the order appealed against his immediately subordinate."



A series of questions were asked by the appellant in his 20 RTI applications
relating to appeals filed under the above rule. However, he was not specific in
respect of the points in particular files on which he need specific information, but
kept on arguing the same stressing that his representations addressed to the
President of India and the Prime Minister of India were erroneously disposed by
the respondents.



Based on a perusal of the record, it was noted that the appellant in his appeal in
case no. 154406 mentioned that a complaint was filed by him to the Controller
Communication Accounts on 31.03.2015, in which he mentioned about the
inability to bring his private vehicle from Ahmedabad to Bhopal, which was not
disposed of within 3 months from its filing and being aggrieved with the same he
filed an appeal under the CCS rules to the Hon'ble President of India on
24.07.2015. He further mentioned in his appeal that his appeal dated 24.07.2015
 being not disposed of till 09.11.2016, he filed a complaint before the Hon'ble
Prime Minister of India on 10.11.2016 which was registered in the CPGRAM portal
as PMOPG/E/2016/0337821. He further submitted that his complaint was
disposed of by CCA, Madhya Pradesh Telecom Circle, Shri Shankar Lal Ji Bhalotiya
on 30.09.2016 due to which he filed a complaint to the Hon'ble PM on 10.11.2017
(which was his 14th complaint letter). Furthermore, the said complaint was
registered as PMOPG/E/2017/0496389 which was also incorrectly disposed of by
Shri Shankar Lal Ji Bhalotiya on 21.09.2016, in respect of which the appellant was
aggrieved and the first complaint addressed to the President of India was sent on
24.10.2017 which was registered as PRSEC/E/2017/14593 which was disposed of
by Shri Satish Kumar, FAA on 22.02.2018. Hence he filed a RTI application on
29.05.2018.

On perusal of the replies provided in the cases, it was noted that each of the
applications were replied to by the CPIO. In respect of the RTI application dated
29.05.2018, the then CPIO replied on 13.07.2018 and stated that 4 RTI
applications were received from the appellant, and the information sought was
scattered    in various files, the disclosure of the same would require
disproportionate diversion of the resources of the public authority, hence he
requested the appellant to come for inspection of the documents with two days
prior notice. The appellant contested the reply and filed a first appeal on
23.07.2018 which was disposed of on 02.08.2018.

It was submitted by the appellant in case no. 157960 that he had filed a complaint
on 28.06.2015 regarding difference in payment of arrears. As the same was not
disposed of on 09.08.2015, he filed an appeal under CCS Rules on 10.08.2015
followed with second appeal to the President on 10.11.2017 which was registered
as PRSEC/E/2017/15354 which was disposed by the FAA incorrectly on
22.02.2018. He mentioned that on same ground he filed second appeal in case
no. 1 on 04.09.2018. He filed another RTI application on 02.06.2018 to which the
same reply was given.

Case nos .632621 and 632623 are the same as case no. 154406. Case nos. 157960
and 632712 are similar, case nos. 632799, 166085 are similar, case nos. 632943,
633330 are also on the similar subject matter, case nos. 634013, 634014 are the
same, case nos. 634383, 170570 are the same, case nos. 634756, 171772 are the
 same, case nos. 635808, 172202 are the same, and case nos. 637063, 173150 are
the same. It is seen from a perusal of the second appeals that these cases involve
repeated and vexatious RTI applications which are numerous and are seeking
same or similar information with regard to the appellant's complaints/Appeals to
the PMO and the President's office and the Advisor Finance from Telecom circle
Bhopal.



It is important to mention here that the CPIO and the FAA both had provided
replies. Seeing the content of the RTI applications and the queries sought , it is
quite clear that these kinds of RTI applications are a complete misuse of the
provisions of the 
RTI Act
 and in the garb of seeking information the appellant is
trying to redress his personal grievance related to service matters and the
disiplinary case against him.It is also important to mention that providing replies
to so many similar RTI applications amounts to harassment of the public
authority and hinders the core function of the public authority. Moreover, the
matter is also not related to any larger public interest but is simply a personal
grievance of the appellant and hence it cannot be expected that to satisfy the
query of a single applicant the public authority should drain their resources by
focussing and answering his numerous similar RTI applications which would
deprive other citizens of their RTIs being responded to. The appellant is also
exhausting the other fora available such as approaching PMO through CPGRAMS
portal, President of India and CAT. It is noted that he is using every possible
avenue to harass the public authority including filing multiple RTI applications in
quick succession hardly allowing the respondent authority to deal with their day
to day work.



Based on a perusal of the records, the Commission observed that the nature of
these RTI Applications are frivolous and in the nature of repetition of similar
issuesand it is also observed that each RTI Application has been replied to. It
appears that the Appellant has grossly misconceived the idea of exercising his
right to Information as being absolute and unconditional and is rather resorting to
misuse of this Act to settle some personal grievance against the respondent
 authorities. In this context, the Commission finds it pertinent to rely on the
## following decisions:


The Hon'ble Supreme Court's observation in Central Board of Secondary
Education(CBSE) & anr. v. Aditya Bandhopadhyay and others [(2011) 8 SCC 497]
## held that:


             "37. The right to information is a cherished right. Information and
             rightto information are intended to be formidable tools in the hands
             of responsible citizens to fight corruption and to bring in transparency
             and accountability. The provisions of 
RTI Act
 should be enforced
             strictly and all efforts should be made to bring to light the necessary
             information under clause (b) of 
section 4(1)
 of the Act which relates
             to securing transparency and accountability in the working of public
             authorities and in discouraging corruption. But in regard to other
             information,(that is information other than those enumerated in
             
section 4(1)(b)
 and (c) of the Act), equal importance and emphasis
             are given to other public interests(like confidentiality of sensitive
             information, fidelity and fiduciary relationships, efficient operation of
             governments, etc.). Indiscriminate and impractical demands or
             directions under 
RTI Act
 for disclosure of all and sundry information
             (unrelated to transparency and accountability in the functioning of
             public authorities and eradication of corruption) would be counter-
             productive as it will adversely affect the efficiency of the
             administration and result in the executive getting bogged down with
             the non- productive work of collecting and furnishing information.
             
The Act
 should not be allowed to be misused or abused, to become a
             tool to obstruct the national development and integration, or to
             destroy the peace, tranquility and harmony among its citizens. Nor
             should it be converted into a tool of oppression or intimidation of
             honest officials striving to do their duty. The nation does not want a
             scenario where 75% of the staff of public authorities spends 75% of
             their time in collecting and furnishing information to applicants
             instead of discharging their regular duties. The threat of penalties
             under the 
RTI Act
 and the pressure of the authorities under the 
RTI
                Act
 should not lead to employees of a public authorities prioritising
               'information furnishing', at the cost of their normal and regular
               duties."

Similarly, in ICAI v. Shaunak H. Satya, (2011) 8 SCC781 the Hon'ble Supreme Court
has held that:-

               "39. We however agree that it is necessary to make a distinction
               inregard to information intended to bring transparency, to
               improveaccountability and to reduce corruption, falling under
               
Sections 4(1)(b)
 and (c) and other information which may not have a
               bearing onaccountability or reducing corruption. The competent
               authorities under the 
RTI Act
 will have to maintain a proper balance
               so that while achieving transparency, the demand for information
               does not reach unmanageable proportions affecting other public
               interests, which include efficient operation of public authorities and
               the Government, preservation of confidentiality of sensitive
               information and optimum use of limited fiscal resources."

In the matter of Rajni Maindiratta- Vs Directorate of Education (North West -
B)[W.P.(C) No. 7911/2015] the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi has held that:

               "8. Though undoubtedly, the reason for seeking the information is
               notrequired to be disclosed but when it is found that the process of
               the law is being abused, the same become relevant. Neither the
               authorities created under the 
RTI Act
 nor the Courts are helpless if
               witness the provisions of law being abused and owe a duty to
               immediately put a stop thereto."

In the matter of Shail Sahni vs Sanjeev Kumar [W.P.(C) 845/2014] wherein the
## Hon'ble High Court of Delhi has held that:


               "This Court is also of the view that misuse of the 
RTI Act
 has to be
               appropriately dealt with, otherwise the public would lose faith and
               confidence in this "sunshine Act". A beneficent Statute, when made a
               tool for mischief and abuse must be checked in accordance with
               law."

The appellant should know that the 
RTI Act
 is a means to promote public interest
and should not to be used as an instrument to harass the public authority or
 settle personal grievances for which he should approach the grievance redressal
forum in the organization. His multiple RTI applications have a grave impact on
the functioning of Department of Telecommunication and if this is allowed, the
public authority cannot focus on their core duties and their entire time will be
devoted to such frivolous/vexatious/ repeated/multiple RTI questions. This is
undoubtedly misuse and it has to be checked. He cannot use the RTI route to
satisfy his personal vengeance, after flooding the public authority with frivolous
RTIs based on the repetitive appeals filed to the President of India and the
## complaints filed to the PMO.


## Decision:


The Commission has clubbed the above referred matters for adjudication as the
subject matter of all these matters pertain to similar grievances of the Appellant.
The nature of queries in all of these matters is such that largely seeks information
regarding action taken on his multiple representations etc addressed to the
President of India, Prime Minister of India and Department of
Telecommunication. These Appeals reveal that the appellant in the garb of
seeking information is flooding the public authority with similar RTI applications.
The issue largely relates to the disiplinary action taken against him by the
department for various acts of indiscipline as contained in the charges mentioned
against him.

The earlier bench of the Commission in a similar case of a different party in File
## No.CIC/BS/A/2014/002319-SA held as follows:


       "16. Filing of multiple RTI on the same subject creates fear among the public
       authority. The feel tormented by such disgruntled/ retired employees
       consuming through RTI their precious resource apart from causing mental
       agony...."

A coordinate bench of the Commission in the matter of Ex MWO S P Bajpai vs.
CPIO, Dept of Personal Services vide decision dated 24.10.2018 held as follows:

       "Upon a conjoint reading of the above dicta and the perusal of facts on
       record, it is established well beyond reasonable doubt that Appellant is in a
       habit of misusing his right to information. Commission advises the
       Appellant to make judicious use of the cherished statute of 
RTI Act
 in future.
       CPIO is advised to deal with any future RTI Applications of the Appellant on
         the subject of grant of Honorary Commission or anything related to service
        related grievance emanating from this subject in accordance with the
        aforesaid observations of the Commission."

Therefore, without commenting on the merits of the appeals, the Commission
deems it appropriate to dismiss these appeals. The Commission hereby rejects all
the contentions of the appellant and orders no relief whatsoever in the matter.
From the averments of the appellant, it is rather apparent that his only intention
was to harass the public authority as well as to waste the time and resources of
the respondents and the Commission. He is therefore cautioned to refrain from
filing such frivolous RTI applications on the same subject matter time and again
and rather use the RTI act wisely and appropriately. In case any such repeated
second appeals or complaints on the same or similar subject matter is filed
before the Commission, the same shall be dismissed in limine.



               The appeals are disposed of accordingly.
