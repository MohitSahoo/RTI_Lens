## Information sought

## :


The Appellant filed an RTI application dated 05.12.2020 seeking the following
## information:




                                          1


 The CPIO denied information to the appellant on 09.12.2020 under 
Section 8(1)(d)

of the RTI Act, 2005.



Being dissatisfied, the appellant filed a First Appeal dated 19.12.2020. FAA's order
dated 05.02.2021 held as under:-



       "...........To verify the contention of the applicant that disclosure of
documents/material is related to economic loss to the Government and therefore
in favour of larger public interest, the survey documents alongwith all the relevant
folders were obtained from the jurisdictional AO. It has been observed that on
01.01.2016 all the impounded material were released by AO in all the 3 cases
referred above vide order dated 01.01.2016. Therefore, as on today, No details or
information can be provided to the applicant being not in possession of the
department. It is also worth mentioning here that from the releasing., order itself,
It is apparent that the applicant Mr. Manoj Saini himself was the witness while
releasing the impounded material to the assessee. In view of the facts discussed
above, the appeal of the applicant for the point number one is dismissed.



       6. Further, in the second point the applicant has asked to provide the Name,
Designation, Mobile No. and address of the officers who conducted survey on the
above referred assesses. However, during hearing of appeal, applicant has failed
to establish that disclosure of the name, address mobile number of officers of the
IT department who were the part of survey team will be in the larger public
interest, so it is held that the request of the applicant on 2116 point was rightly
denied by the Public Information Officer i.e. ITO Kishangarli under the rule 8 (1)d
of the RIT Act which is reproduced as under.

 "The requisite information may not be disclosed as the requisite information to
third party, is exempt from disclosure. "Section 8(1)(d) of the RIT Act."

                                         2


 Feeling aggrieved and dissatisfied, appellant approached the Commission with the
instant Second Appeal.



## Relevant Facts emerging during Hearing:


The following were present:-



Appellant: Present through video conference.

Respondent: Balvir Singh Tanwar, ITO, Ward-1 & CPIO present through video
conference.



The CPIO submitted that the FAA had clarified in the order that the impounded
documents were released to the concerned assessee, so the question of
availability of the same with their office does not arise. He further reiterated the
denial of the information on point no.2 of the RTI Application.



The Appellant insisted that the department ought to have maintained copy of the
impounded documents, to which the CPIO affirmed that no copy is retained as it
serves no purpose.



## Decision:




The Commission based on a perusal of the facts on record observes that the
inadequacies in the CPIO's reply has been addressed by the FAA in his order
wherein the unavailability of the information sought for at point no.1 of the RTI
Application has been explained.



As for point no.2 of the RTI Application, the Commission observes that the
exemption of 
Section 8(1)(d)
 of the RTI Act appears irrelevant and has not been
substantiated by the CPIO. However, pertinently so, disclosure of the details of
the survey committee members stands exempted under 
Section 8(1)(j)
 & (g) of
the 
RTI Act
. 
In this regard, the attention of the Appellant is drawn towards a
judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the matter of Central Public
Information Officer, Supreme Court of 
India Vs. Subhash Chandra Agarwal
 in
Civil Appeal No. 10044 of 2010 with Civil Appeal No. 10045 of 2010 and Civil
## Appeal No. 2683 of 2010 wherein the import of "personal information" envisaged

under 
Section 8(1)(j)
 of RTI Act has been exemplified in the context of earlier
ratios 
laid down by
 the same Court in the matter(
s) of Canara Bank Vs. C.S.
Shyam
 in Civil Appeal No.22 of 2009; 
Girish Ramchandra Deshpande vs. Central

                                         3

 Information Commissioner & Ors.
, (2013) 1 SCC 212 and R.K. Jain vs. Union of
India & Anr., (2013) 14 SCC 794. The following was thus held:




       "59. Reading of the aforesaid judicial precedents, in our opinion, would
indicate that personal records, including name, address, physical, mental and
psychological status, marks obtained, grades and answer sheets, are all treated as
personal information. Similarly, professional records, including qualification,
performance, evaluation reports, ACRs, disciplinary proceedings, etc. are all
personal information. Medical records, treatment, choice of medicine, list of
hospitals and doctors visited, findings recorded, including that of the family
members, information relating to assets, liabilities, income tax returns, details of
investments, lending and borrowing, etc. are personal information. Such personal
information is entitled to protection from unwarranted invasion of privacy and
conditional access is available when stipulation of larger public interest is
satisfied. This list is indicative and not exhaustive..."



             With the above observations, the appeal is disposed of.
