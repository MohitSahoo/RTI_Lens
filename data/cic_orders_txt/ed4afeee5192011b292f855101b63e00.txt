Information sought
:



The Appellant sought a copy of the speaking order passed in the case of Shri L.V.
Nilesh, IAS (MH:91) in continuation of his earlier RTI Application dated

02.08.2017.



                                        1


 The CPIO, informed the Appellant on 17.01.2018 that the RTI application does not
pertain to their department. However, CPIO provided a copy of the reply dated
17.08.2017 sent by the CPIO, AIS(III)in reference to his earlier RTI Application.



Being dissatisfied, the appellant filed a First Appeal on 20.10.2017. FAA, vide
order dated 09.08.2018, upheld the reply of CPIO and disposed of his appeal.



Grounds for the Second Appeal:

Feeling aggrieved and dissatisfied, Appellant approached the Commission with
the instant Second Appeal.



Relevant Facts emerging during Hearing:

The following were present:-



Appellant: Not present.

Respondent: S K Singh, CPIO, S K Sinha, US & CPIO and Ram Lakhan, SO present
through intra-video conference.



The CPIO submitted that the Appellant was duly informed that the information
sought for is not available with their office. He further stated that the FAA had
also clarified to the Appellant that no such speaking order is available in their
records and the available record of the deemed resignation of L V Nilesh has
already been provided to the Appellant.



The Appellant did not avail of the opportunity to attend the hearing, however, he
has sent his written submissions wherein he has stated that paras 9 & 10 of his
grounds of Appeal may be considered as his arguments during the hearing. Para 9
& 10 of the Appeal state as under:



       "The document I asked for through my RTI application dated 3l August 2017
is: 'Copy of the speaking order passed in the case of L. V. Nilesh, IAS (MH: 91)'. This
document concerns me because the government has referred to this very case in
passing the deemed-to-have-resigned order against me (Annexure XVIL). As
mentioned earlier in this appeal, DoPT through their letter dated 17 August 2017
provided a 4-line non-speaking order (Annexure XVIID, which was not what I had
asked for. That's the reason I mentioned 'speaking order' in bold in my second RTI
application.



      I want to raise few points here:

                                          2


        l. Is this information not available with the DoPT? Has the department not
passed any speaking order anywhere - letter or file noting - where it evaluated
evidence before passing the order against Mr. L. V. Nilesh. A notification isn't
issued just on its own without any foundational reasoning to support it.

       2. As mentioned in this appeal earlier, there was some back and forth
correspondence between different sections of DoPT regarding which section keeps
such documents. If the CPIO didn't possess these documents (she has mentioned
this in her order), it was her duty to direct the application to the appropriate
section. It's not possible for me to know which among many sections in DoPT
would possess such documents.

       3. The First Appellate Authority has mentioned in her order that the CPIO
has already furnished the information'. It has not been. The information asked was
a speaking order and what was provided was merely the final notification.

       4. The documents asked for are related to my case of deemed resignation. I
have appealed against Government's order for deemed resignation in CAT (O.A.
No. 22 of 2014) and I need these documents to support my case. This particular
case is grave because the same department that passed the deemed resignation
order is the arbiter in releasing information that can potentially go against it."



Decision

The Commission observes from a perusal of the facts on record that the Appellant
is insisting upon a record which is held to be not available with the public
authority. It is the contention of the Appellant that what he is asking for may not
be strictly a speaking order, it could be a letter or file noting, and the CPIO should
find out the availability of the same and provide it to him.



The Commission finds the contentions of the Appellant untenable as under the
mandate of the 
RTI Act
, the CPIO cannot be compelled to interpret or deduce the
records as only information that is readily available on record can be provided.
Moreover, the merits of the Appellant's contentions do not warrant any further
determination as the information sought for in the RTI Application pertains to the
service record of a third party, without according any larger public interest in the
disclosure of said information and therefore, the same is exempt under 
Section
8(1)(j)
 of the RTI Act. The said observation is in line with a judgment of the
Hon'ble Supreme Court in the matter of Central Public Information Officer,
Supreme Court of 
India Vs. Subhash Chandra Agarwal
 in Civil Appeal No. 10044
of 2010 with Civil Appeal No. 10045 of 2010 and Civil Appeal No. 2683 of 2010
wherein the import of "personal information" envisaged under 
Section 8(1)(j)
 of

                                          3


RTI Act
 has been exemplified in the context of earlier ratios 
laid down by
 the
same Court in the matter(
s) of Canara Bank Vs. C.S. Shyam
 in Civil Appeal No.22
of 2009; 
Girish Ramchandra Deshpande vs. Central Information Commissioner &
Ors.
, (2013) 1 SCC 212 and R.K. Jain vs. Union of India & Anr., (2013) 14 SCC 794.




In view of the foregoing observations, the Commission finds the instant Appeal
bereft of merit and upholds the submissions of the CPIO.



The appeal is disposed of accordingly.
