                                  ORDER

1.   The appellant filed an application under the 
Right to Information Act, 2005


(RTI Act
) before the Central Public Information Officer (CPIO), National Thermal
Power Corporation Limited, New Delhi seeking following information:-

     "1. Provide copy of Training policy for Executives and effective as
     on date in NTPC.

     2. Provide Name and designation of Training Co-ordinator of Coal
     Mining HQ Noida.

     3. Provide basis for nomination of executives for attending training.

     4. Provide minimum no of mandatory training prescribed for each
     level of executive in a year.

     5. Provide Conference & Seminars and Training detail taken outside
     NTPC offices wherein executives posted at Coal Mining-HQ
     (Corporate Centre) were nominated to attend, highlighting name of
     employee, description of training, location/venue of such training for
     the calendar years 2017-18.

     6. Provide executive wise Training/ Conference/ Seminar expenses
     w.r.t point 5 supra, incurred by NTPC towards Taxis, Airfare


                                                                        Page 1 of 4

         charges, hotel expenses, Conference fee and any other associated
        costs for the calendar years 2017-18."

2.      The CPIO responded on 03-01-2019. The appellant filed the first appeal
dated 18-02-2019 which was disposed of by the first appellate authority on 27-02-
2019. Thereafter, he filed a second appeal u/
Section 19(3)
 of the RTI Act before
the Commission requesting to take appropriate legal action against the CPIO
u/
Section 20
 of the RTI Act, 2005 and also to direct him to provide the sought for
information.

Hearing:

3.      The appellant, Mr. Atul Kumar Jhunjhuwala attended the hearing through
audio conferencing. Mr. Shantimoy Nandan, CPIO and Mr. Pratik Roy, Senior
Manager participated in the hearing representing the respondent through audio
conferencing. The written submissions are taken on record.

4.      The appellant limited his submissions on providing the break-up of the
expenditure incurred by the executives of the NTPC on attending the
training/conference/seminar.

5.      The respondent submitted that the information in the manner asked by the
appellant on point no. 6 requires compilation which if undertaken would lead to
diversion of resources of the public authority. Therefore, they have invoked

Section 7(9)
 of the RTI Act, 2005. However, they have furnished the point-wise
information w.r.t. rest of the points.

Decision:

6.      This Commission observes that it would not be feasible for the respondent to
furnish the information on point no. 6 thereby indicating the executive wise
training/conference/seminar expenses incurred towards taxis, airfare charges, hotel
bills, conference fee and any other associated costs for the calendar years 2017-18,
as it is readily not available in a compiled form. Compilation of such data would
lead to diversion of resources of the public authority which is not the spirit of the

RTI Act, 2005
. In this regard, the Commission observes that the CPIO cannot be
expected to compile the information which requires disproportionate diversion of
the resources of the public authority. This Commission takes note of the provisions
of 
Section 7(9)
 of the RTI Act, 2005 which reads as under:-

            "(9) An information shall ordinarily be provided in the form in
            which it is sought unless it would disproportionately divert the
            resources of the public authority or would be detrimental to the
            safety or preservation of the record in question."



                                                                           Page 2 of 4


 
7.     On this aspect, it is apt to mention the decision of the Hon'ble Supreme
Court of India in Civil Appeal No. 6454 of 2011 dated 09/08/2011 titled as

Central Board of Secondary Education & Anr. v. Aditya Bandopadhyay &
Ors.
, wherein, it was observed as under:-


                    "Indiscriminate and impractical demands or directions
             under 
RTI Act
 for disclosure of all and sundry information
             (unrelated to transparency and accountability in the functioning
             of public authorities and eradication of corruption) would be
             counter-productive as it will adversely affect the efficiency of
             the administration and result in the executive getting bogged
             down with the non productive work of collecting and
             furnishing information. 
The Act
 should not be allowed to be
             misused or abused, to become a tool to obstruct the national
             development and integration, or to destroy the peace,
             tranquillity and harmony among its citizens. Nor should it be
             converted into a tool of oppression or intimidation of honest
             officials striving to do their duty. The nation does not want a
             scenario where 75% of the staff of public authorities spends
             75% of their time in collecting and furnishing information to
             applicants instead of discharging their regular duties. The
             threat of penalties under the 
RTI Act
 and the pressure of the
             authorities under the 
RTI Act
 should not lead to employees of a
             public authorities prioritizing 'information furnishing', at the
             cost of their normal and regular duties."

8.     In light of the factual matrix of the case and the legal principles enunciated
in the aforesaid case-law, this Commission comes to the conclusion that the CPIO
is not obliged to compile any fresh data which entails disproportionate diversion of
the resources of the public authority. Hence, no further intervention of the
Commission is required in the matter.

9.     With the above observations, the appeal is disposed of.
