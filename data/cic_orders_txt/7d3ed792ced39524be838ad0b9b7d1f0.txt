1.        The issues under consideration arising out of the second appeal dated 13.02.2019
include non-receipt of the following information raised by the appellant through his RTI
application dated 28.11.2018 and first appeal dated 28.12.2018:-

     i.   Copies of the Life Certificates furnished by the Family Pensioner from the year 2002
          onwards.

 ii.      Information about action taken to cause a discreet inquiry and to take action against the
          impostor and her accomplices as well who were involved in posthumous withdrawal of
          the pension of Smt. Barkatunnisa Begum who died on 30.08.2008.
iii.      Copy of the inquiry report supported by action taken report.
 iv.      Names, designations and complete addresses of all those officers who processed
          payment of family pension of Smt. Barkatunnisa Begum after 01.09.2008.



                                                                                         Page 1 of 5


  v.    Reasons on record for such an inordinate delay in conduct of inquiry or identifying the
       impostor, her accomplices or officers & others who were involved in fake claim of
       posthumous pension complained of.

2.     Succinctly facts of the case are that the appellant filed an application dated 28.11.2018
under the 
Right to Information Act, 2005
 
(RTI Act
) before the Central Public Information
Officer (CPIO), Union Bank of India, Nashik, Maharashtra, seeking aforesaid information. The
CPIO did not reply. Dissatisfied due to non receipt of any response from the CPIO, the
appellant has filed first appeal dated 28.12.2018. The First Appellate Authority (FAA) did not
pass any order. Aggrieved by this, the appellant has filed a second appeal dated 13.02.2019
before the Commission which is under consideration.



3.     The appellant has filed the instant appeal dated 13.02.2019 inter alia on the grounds that
desired information was not provided by the CPIO. The appellant has requested the
Commission to direct the CPIO to provide information immediately and take necessary action
as per sub-section (1) of 
section 20
 of the RTI Act.

4.     Perusal of the documents submitted by the appellant for filing the instant appeal reveals
that the CPIO and the FAA did not reply to the RTI application as well as the first appeal.
Hearing on 19.03.2021:

4.1.   The appellant on behalf of the respondent Shri Sanjay Lakra, Assistant General Manager,
Union Bank of India, Nashik, attended the hearing through video conference.



4.2.   The Commission passed the following directions on 07.06.2021:



       "6.    The Commission after adverting to the facts and circumstances of the case,
       hearing both the parties and perusal of records, observes that information sought was
       not provided by the respondent till the date of hearing. Though, in the FAA's order dated
       26.02.2019, it was mentioned that the CPIO had already replied to the appellant vide
       letter dated 22.10.2018. Perusal of the CPIO's reply, reveals that information sought
       was denied under 
section 8 (1) (d)
 and (e) of the 
RTI Act
. However, the respondent failed
       to justify as to how disclosure of such information would be exempted under the
       provisions as claimed by the CPIO. The appellant being an affected party entitled for the
       information sought by him. It may not be out of place to mention that banks are supposed
       to be transparent and accountable to the public at large. Accordingly, the respondent


                                                                                        Page 2 of 5

        may revisit the RTI application and revised point-wise information/reply may be made
       available to the appellant within three weeks from the date of receipt of this order.
       Further, Shri Ajit V. Marathe, the present CPIO and Shri Vinayak Temburne, the then
       CPIO, are show caused as to why maximum penalty should not be imposed upon each of
       them as per 
section 20 (1)
 of RTI Act for not providing proper reply/information to the
       appellant. The present CPIO is under obligation to serve copy of this order -cum -show
       cause notice to the then CPIO and secure his explanation. All the written explanations
       (from both the CPIOs) including copy of the revised reply must reach the Commission
       within 21 days from the date of receipt of this order."



Hearing on 07.09.2021


5.     The appellant remained absent and on behalf of the respondent Shri Vinayak Temburne,
the then CPIO, Union Bank of India, Nashik attended the hearing through video conference.



5.2.   The respondent while defending their case inter alia submitted that written explanations
in response to the show cause notices were submitted by the CPIOs vide letter dated
30.06.2021. The respondent further submitted that the information being related to third parties
in which criminal acts were also involved, the information was denied under 
section 8 (1) (d)

and (e) of the 
RTI Act
. However, in compliance of the Commission's order dated 07.06.2021
they had provided the requisite information and that their earlier decision was an error of
judgment. The point-wise information was provided to the appellant on 29.06.2021 and the
same is reproduced as under:



       "i. We wish to inform you that the life certificates are not available with our parbhani
       branch

       ii. The bank has identified the imposter/accused Mr.Mahmood Bin Awaz Bamar and Mrs.
       Badrunissa Begum Mubarak Bamar. Further, the bank has also filed FIR against the
       accused with Modha Police Station Parbhani

       iii. Bank had lodged FIR No. 0360/2019 with Modha Police station, Parbhani and the
       copy of the same can be obtained from the police authority or the Hon'ble court

       iv. We wish to inform that the information sought by you is not readily available and
       compiled by the bank. Hence, as per 
section 7 (9)
 of the RTI Act the compilation of the


                                                                                      Page 3 of 5

       said information from 01.09.2008 would disproportionately divert the resources of the
      bank.



      v. You have sought reasons and the same does not fall under the definition of information
      under 
section 2 (f)
 of the Act. However, we would like to inform you that the bank had
      identified the imposters as soon as cheating by personation came into bank's knowledge
      and further FIR was duly lodged against the police."



6.    The Commission after adverting to the facts and circumstances of the case, hearing
the respondent and perusal of records, observed that the respondent complied with the
Commission's order dated 07.06.2021 and provided revised information on 29.06.2021.
Further, it may be noted that the respondent had denied the information earlier, however,
in absence of mala fide it would not be appropriate to take penal action against the CPIO
for an error in judgment. Further, the explanations submitted by the respondents were
reasonable and it appeared that there was no conscious and deliberate attempt to
withhold the information. In absence of any mala fide on the part of the CPIOs and due
reply having been provided, there appears to be no reason for taking penal action against
the CPIOs. Therefore, the show cause notices issued to Shri Ajit V. Marathe, the present
CPIO and Shri Vinayak Temburne, the then CPIO, are hereby dropped. However, it may
not be out of place to record that allegations have been made of posthumous withdrawal
from pension account and negligence/involvement of bank staff has been overlooked
without referring thereto. The CPIOs/officers may be sensitized to be careful in future.
With the above observations and directions, the appeal is disposed of.
