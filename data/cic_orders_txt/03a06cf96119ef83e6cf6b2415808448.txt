Decision

1. Since the moot question of law is the same in all of the above cases, they are
decided by a common decision. Upon perusal of records of the cases and hearing
averments of the parties, first and foremost it is noted that the Appellant has
repeatedly averred through his various submissions that directions be given to
the public authority to furnish information as sspecified
                                                  pecified by the Appellant from
the DCP, Hq. and DCP, PCR, though such information is available on the
website and held by relevant area police stations. The Commission wishes to
point out that information which is already in public domain does not strictly
fall within the purview of "information" which needs to be "provided" under the

RTI Act
 by the public authority, because such information is already disclosed in





                                                                       Page 22 of 28

 the public domain. This issue has been dealt with in various decisions from time
to time, some of which are as follows:



    i) Shri Ram Singh vs. Central Public Information Officer (Decision No.
    CIC/YA/A/2014/000379/SB) dated 19th September 2016: "
The RTI
    Act
 mandates every public authority to provide as much information to
    the public including through internet so that the public have minimum
    resort to the use of the 
RTI Act
 to obtain information. Further, once
    information has been provided in public domain and on website then
    the information is no longer held by or under the control of any public
    authority and hence, is no longer accessible as 'right to information'.

    Thus, the action of the CPIO is consonance with the 
RTI Act
. The
    Commission, further observes that under the 
RTI Act
 only information
    available on records has to be provided, which has already been
    furnished to the appellant by the respondent."

                                                    Emphasis supplied

 Further, the CIC decision dated 12th April 2007 in K. Lall vs. M.K. Bagri (File
 No. CIC/AT/A/2007/00112) held as follows:



   "...
Section 2(j)
 of the RTI Act speaks of "the right to information accessible
   under this Act which is held by or under the control of any public
   authority...". "...The use of the words "accessible under this Act"; "held
   by" and "under the control of'' are crucial in this regard. The inference
   from the text of this sub-section and, especially the three expressions
   quoted above, is that an information to which a citizen will have a right
   should be shown to be a) an information which is accessible under the
   
RTI Act
 and b) that it is held or is under the control of a certain public
   authority. This should mean that unless an information is exclusively held
   and controlled by a public authority, that information cannot be said to be
   an information accessible under the 
RTI Act
. Inferentially it would mean
   that once a certain information is placed in the public domain accessible
   to the citizens either freely, or on payment of a pre-determined price, that
   information cannot be said to be 'held' or 'under the control of' the public
   authority and, thus would cease to be an information accessible under the
   
RTI Act
. This interpretation is further strengthened by the provisions of the
   
RTI Act
 in 
Sections 4(2)
, 
4(3)
 and 
4(4)
..."

                                                            Emphasis supplied


2. The Appellant averred during hearing and through his written submissions
that he seeks information i) as published on website and ii) from the office of
DCP, Hq. and Police Control Room not from the police stations because it is his





                                                                         Page 23 of 28

 contention that information held by police stations is not accurate. The
Respondents stated that information sought by the Appellant is not maintained
by the Headquarter or the Control Room, but actual custodians of such
information are the respective area police stations, who have duly furnished the
information held by them. In view of the factual position clearly stated by the
Respondent, the contention of the Appellant is clearly not maintainable.
Considering the admitted factual position wherein information sought by the
Appellant had been duly provided by the actual custodian of information viz. the
relevant police stations or is already published on the website, the Appellant's
plea that the information should be available or compiled by the specific offices
of DCP, Hq and DCP, PCR does not appear to be in sync with the provisions of
the 
RTI Act
 nor can such direction be issued under the 
RTI Act
.



 
The Commission finds it pertinent to place reliance on a decision of the Hon'ble
 High Court of Gujarat in the case of 
Arvindlal Ambalal Patel vs. State of Gujarat
 and Ors.
 in Special Civil Appeal No. 12223/2015 decided on 31.07.2015 which
 is pertinent on this issue, which reads as follows:




       "...the prayers prayed for in this petition cannot be granted. There
    cannot be any action in a matter like the present one by the respondent
    authorities pursuant to an RTI application, at the instance of a person
    like the present petitioner. At the most, under the RTI proceedings, the
    petitioner can ask for some information, but, cannot demand any
    action.."

 
Emphasis supplied

  It is also noteworthy that the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India in the matter of
 
Union of India v. Namit Sharma
 in Review Petition [C] No.2309/2012 in Writ
 Petition [C] No.210 of 2012 with State of Rajasthan and Anr. vs. Namit Sharma
 Review Petition [C] No.2675 of 2012 In Writ Petition [C] No.210 OF 2012 had
 also held as under:




    "While deciding whether a citizen should or should not get a particular
    information "which is held by or under the control of any public
    authority", the Information Commission does not decide a dispute
    between two or more parties concerning their legal rights other than
    their right to get information in possession of a public authority. This
    function obviously is not a judicial function, but an administrative
    function conferred by the Act on the Information Commissions."

4. Thus perusal of the records, averments of parties and the above discussion
clearly establishes the fact that "information" as defined under 
Section 2(f)
 of the
RTI Act, i.e. information held or readily available with the public authority has
been provided by the respective custodians of information, viz. North West
District, Crime Branch, Vigilance Branch, Dwarka District etc. The list of 178
persons arrested during the relevant period is already available with the
Appellant as is the list of nine arrested persons specifically provided by ADCP,
Dwarka Distt., vide letter dated 12.03.2020. It is also an undisputed fact that
the Appellant has been offered inspection of records in some cases and he has
been informed appropriately wherever records have been destroyed as per
Record Retention Schedule.



5. It is noted that the Appellant has placed reliance on a decision of the Delhi
High Court in the case of 
Ankit Kapoor vs. The Income Tax Officer, Ward
 634
&Anr., which deals with inconvenience faced by the litigants at ITAT due to non
publication of daily ordersheets as well as the revised cause list on the website
by the ITAT. The facts of the case cited by the Appellant are found irrelevant and
inapplicable to the facts of the abovementioned 12 cases filed by him.




6. The Appellant while admitting that the police stations have provided the
desired information to him, insisted that the same should be compiled and made
available at the office of DCP, Hq. and the DCP, PCR. It is in this context that
the Apex Court in the decision dated 09.08.2011 in the case titled: 
Central
Board Of Secondary Education & Anr. vs Aditya Bandopadhyay & Ors.
 is found
squarely applicable, wherein it has been discussed that:




   "... 34. When trying to ensure that the right to information does not
   conflict with several other public interests (which includes efficient
   operations of the governments, preservation of confidentiality of sensitive
   information, optimum use of limited fiscal resources, etc.), ...



                                                                            Page 25 of 28


     35. At this juncture, it is necessary to clear some misconceptions about
    the 
RTI Act
. 
The RTI Act
 provides access to all information that is available
    and existing. This is clear from a combined reading of section 3 and the
    definitions of `information' and `right to information' under clauses (f) and

    (j) of 
section 2
 of the Act. If a public authority has any information in the
    form of data or analysed data, or abstracts, or statistics, an applicant may
    access such information, subject to the exemptions in 
section 8
 of the Act.

    But where the information sought is not a part of the record of a public
    authority, and where such information is not required to be maintained
    under any law or the rules or regulations of the public authority, the Act
    does not cast an obligation upon the public authority, to collect or collate
    such non- available information and then furnish it to an applicant. A
    public authority is also not required to furnish information which require
    drawing of inferences and/or making of assumptions. It is also not
    required to provide `advice' or `opinion' to an applicant, nor required to
    obtain and furnish any `opinion' or `advice' to an applicant. The reference
    to `opinion' or `advice' in the definition of `information' in 
section 2(f)
 of the
    Act, only refers to such material available in the records of the public
    authority. Many public authorities have, as a public relation exercise,
    provide advice, guidance and opinion to the citizens. But that is purely
    voluntary and should not be confused with any obligation under the 
RTI
    Act
....

     37. ....Indiscriminate and impractical demands or directions under 
RTI
     Act
 for disclosure of all and sundry information (unrelated to
     transparency and accountability in the functioning of public authorities
     and eradication of corruption) would be counter-productive as it will
     adversely affect the efficiency of the administration and result in the
     executive getting bogged down with the non-productive work of collecting
     and furnishing information. 
The Act
 should not be allowed to be misused
     or abused, to become a tool to obstruct the national development and
     integration, or to destroy the peace, tranquility and harmony among its
     citizens. Nor should it be converted into a tool of oppression or
     intimidation of honest officials striving to do their duty. The nation does
     not want a scenario where 75% of the staff of public authorities spends
     75% of their time in collecting and furnishing information to applicants
     instead of discharging their regular duties..."



In the light of the above discussion, the Commission finds no requirement of
intervention of the Commission under 
RTI Act
, in the above-mentioned cases,
since there is no legal infirmity in the replies furnished by the respective
Respondents.

    any merit as the same issue has been dealt with in a multitude of cases.
    Similarly, the Respondent public authority has also spent considerable
    time and resources in responding to the Appellant's RTI Applications.
    Moreover, it is deemed appropriate that the Commission as well as the
    public authority are at liberty to take recourse to the rationale of this order
    whenever the same subject matter is raised by the Appellant in any future
    RTI Applications and/or Page 13 of 13 Appeals. The Appellant is further
    advised not to make a mockery of the spirit of the 
RTI Act
 by
    unnecessarily flooding the public authority with RTI Applications on the
    same matter not involving any larger public interest. It is abundantly clear
    that any number of RTI Applications on the same issue will not alter the
    information that was held and disseminated by the Respondents.



    With the aforesaid observations, the instant Second Appeal is dismissed."



 In view of the above facts, the Appellant is advised to seek appropriate legal
 remedy before the Court where the substantive issue is being adjudicated
 already instead of filing futile, vexatious and frivolous RTI applications thereby
 misusing and abusing the 
RTI Act
 per se. Thus, based on the above mentioned
 observation, the Commission advises the Appellant to refrain from filing multiple
 RTI applications on the same issue essentially related to his personal grievance
 and redressal thereof. Furthermore, it is made clear that any future matter filed
 by the same Appellant on the same subject matter shall be adjudicated based on
 the facts and circumstances of the matter without issuing separate notices of
 hearing.



 The cases are disposed off as such.
